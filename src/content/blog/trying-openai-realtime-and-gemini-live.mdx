*Draft — not final. May be revised.*

OpenAI shipped gpt-realtime-2 on May 7, 2026. Google shipped Gemini 3.1 Flash Live around the same window. Both are speech-to-speech: a single multimodal model takes audio in and produces audio out, with no separate transcribe-LLM-synthesize pipeline in between.

If like me, you're used to talking to Siri or Alexa in a specific "I'm talking to a computer" voice — slowing your cadence, enunciating, waiting for the chime before you start, waiting for the response to finish before you reply — the S2S models are a small revelation. You can start mid-sentence. You can interrupt. You don't have to finish the response before clarifying what you actually meant. Lower latency is part of it; the other part is that barge-in actually works, so the conversational rhythm gets closer to talking to a person than talking to a device.

Caveat upfront: I ran all of this from my apartment on fiber, sitting still in front of a laptop. Real S2S deployment — in a car, on an embedded device, over a flaky LTE connection — probably feels different. The latency I'm describing is the floor case, not the median.

## What I did

I built a small WebRTC harness that runs the same script against three configurations: gpt-realtime-2 (S2S), Gemini Live 3.1 Flash (S2S), and a cascaded baseline (Whisper transcription → GPT-5.1 via Responses at `reasoning_effort: low` → gpt-4o-mini-tts). One UI, one mic, one script, switch the radio button.

The script is a five-turn climbing-trip planning conversation. Each turn stresses a different capability — audio-signal preservation, mid-flight context, domain knowledge with proper nouns, multi-step reasoning, and tool use.

*I chose climbing because I know the domain very well and it can be logistically challenging — perfect for testing reasoning.*

| # | What it tests | What I said |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Audio-signal preservation + self-correction. Does the model track the corrected entity ("Yosemite, no wait Bishop") and does the transcript render Bishop correctly in climbing context? | *"I wanna climb in Yosemite this weekend, no wait Bishop. What's the weather like."* |
| 2 | Mid-flight context, hesitation handling, cross-turn memory. Reverses Turn 1's correction, with filler ("Actually hmm") that energy-VAD pipelines often cut on. | *"Actually hmm I think Bishop will be too hot, but it's kind of hard to get a place to stay in Yosemite."* |
| 3 | Proper nouns + domain knowledge. Does the model name real Forest Service roads or fall back to "check nearby"? | *"Maybe we can find some dispersed camping, what are some good options near the 120 entrance or Tioga pass."* |

Can you look into ticks on Mountain Project to see what the weather's like?"* |

Sessions are recorded as separate user and model audio tracks plus a transcript. Most of the clips below come from a single gpt-realtime-2 run and a matched Gemini Live run on 2026-06-25.

A note on developer effort:

- **Cascaded is roughly 280 lines of TypeScript the S2S models do for you server-side.** A hand-rolled energy VAD, a four-state machine (`idle → recording → processing → speaking`), manual conversation threading across STT, LLM, and TTS endpoints, and tail-echo handling so the model's own audio doesn't re-trigger the VAD. The two S2S setups combined are shorter than that.
- **The defaults are wrong for voice.** My VAD silence threshold was 700ms; it cut the multi-clause back-calc question off mid-sentence on turn 4 and made the model look like it couldn't reason about a question it was only seeing a fragment of. Bumped to 2 seconds. On the output side, GPT-5.1 returns paragraph-length responses by default — I had to add *"Keep responses to 1-3 sentences"* to the cascaded system prompt before it stopped reading me structured five-bullet replies. S2S defaults to voice-appropriate brevity and server-side VAD I never had to tune.
- **The 24-second latency on turn 4 is my naive implementation, not a structural floor.** The pipeline waits for STT to finish, then the full LLM response, then full TTS audio, then plays. A faster build would stream LLM tokens into TTS and start playback on the first chunks — time-to-first-audio on a back-calc-shaped turn could plausibly land at 5–8 seconds instead of 24. But that's *more* cascaded complexity to ship, not less. S2S does it inside the model.

## What worked

Latency and barge-in held up. When I interrupted the model mid-sentence, it stopped cleanly and reformed around what I'd said. The conversational rhythm felt close to real-time. Gemini Live and gpt-realtime-2 were both noticeably better here than the cascaded baseline, which has a 7–14 second per-turn floor in my setup just from STT + LLM + TTS latency stacking.

One smaller thing I didn't expect: the S2S models preserve the audio signal end-to-end, so when the transcript layer mis-hears something, the model can still respond correctly. I'll come back to this — it's the same property that produces the rap/wrap divergence below.

A note on voice character. The default OpenAI voice (I was on Cedar) sounded natural and fairly even — the kind of register you'd pick if you wanted a voice that gets out of the way. Gemini Live's default voice had more going on: noticeably more inflection across the sentence, and a raspy or hoarse quality baked into the voice itself. Not unpleasant, more characterful. Worth flagging because for any consumer voice product, the voice character is half the product — and Gemini and OpenAI are picking different defaults on a spectrum from neutral-narrator to expressive-companion.

## Where the cascaded baseline came out ahead

The cascaded pipeline lost on latency, barge-in, and audio preservation. It won on substance, in different ways on every turn of the script. Worth naming because the post otherwise risks reading as "S2S beats cascaded on everything that matters," and that's not what I saw.

- **Turn 1 (weather).** Cascaded opened with *"I can't access live weather data, but for late June it's typically hot and dry: daytime highs often 85–100°F, cooler nights around 50–60°F… To plan precisely, I'd check the NWS, Weather.gov, or a weather app for 'Bishop, CA'."* It named the limit, gave a seasonal hedge, and pointed at the right source. Neither S2S did any of that — gpt-realtime-2 fabricated specifics, Gemini said "looking into that" without looking.
- **Turn 2 (Yosemite lodging).** Cascaded named alternatives — Tuolumne Meadows, Lover's Leap, Tahoe crags — plus a cancellation strategy (Recreation.gov) and nearby towns (El Portal, Lee Vining, Groveland). The S2S models gave "check nearby" advice with no specifics.
- **Turn 3 (dispersed camping near Hwy 120).** Cascaded named real Forest Service roads — Forest Rd 1N02 / Poole Power Plant Rd, Sawmill Canyon Rd, Hardin Flat / Cherry Lake Rd in Stanislaus National Forest. These check out. S2S gave general "Saddlebag Lake Road / Lee Vining Canyon" — fine but less actionable.
- **Turn 4 (the back-calc).** Cascaded was the only one that correctly differentiated the two descent options *and* named the realistic alternative: *"~6 hours of rappels or a much better 2–3 hours for the downclimb… or do the big route Saturday and keep Sunday lighter."* That last clause is the actually-right answer to the question. Neither S2S got there.
- **Turn 5 (tool use).** Covered in the Turn 5 section below — cascaded gave the cleanest refusal plus the most useful general knowledge and a specific NOAA point forecast to check.

The common thread is the LLM. GPT-5.1 over Responses is text-trained, has stronger calibration on what it doesn't know, and is willing to volunteer specific sources or alternatives when it can't answer the question as asked. The S2S models default to either fabricating specifics or fabricating the action of looking them up. That's a model-training gap, not a structural property of S2S — but it's where the cascaded pipeline still beats both Realtime and Gemini Live on every turn that needs substance more than tempo.

## What still doesn't work

### Both models dropped context inside a six-minute conversation

I'd opened the gpt-realtime-2 session with a weekend trip framing. Three turns later, when I asked "what's the weather in Yosemite?", the model asked me which dates I was planning to be there. I re-stated "this weekend." Recoverable, annoying.

The exact same drop happened on Gemini Live. I opened with "I want to climb in Yosemite this weekend… actually Bishop, what's the weather?" Three turns later I asked Gemini to check lodging in Yosemite and it came back with "What specific dates are you considering for Yosemite?" I had to re-state "this weekend" to it too. Same failure shape, different model.

The second context drop on gpt-realtime-2 wasn't recoverable in the same way. I asked it to plan out when I'd have to leave the Bay Area to get back at 7pm Sunday. After it walked through the climb math, I asked "so when is it?" — the model answered as if I were asking about when to leave the climbing destination, not the Bay Area. Even then, the final answer (leave the climbing area by 2pm) only accounted for the return drive home, not the outbound drive from the Bay to the trailhead. This isn't a model that can't reason. It's a model that commits confidently to a specific answer while quietly skipping a constraint. That's a worse failure mode for the end user than hedging would be — confidently-wrong is harder to catch in a voice interface than confidently-wrong in text, because you can't re-read the answer.

Gemini Live, given the same back-calculation, got further. It did the round-trip math — both the outbound drive from the Bay and the return — which is the structural piece gpt-realtime-2 dropped. But the magnitudes don't close. Gemini quoted "2–3 hours for the downclimb or 12 rappels" — those aren't equivalent (12 rappels at 30 minutes each is 6 hours, not 2–3), and the total mountain-day estimate drops the hike out from the base of the route back to the trailhead. Within Gemini's own range, the late endpoint doesn't work either: leave 5 AM Sunday + 5 hr drive + 12 hr activity + 5 hr drive home = arrive 3 AM Monday, not 7 PM Sunday. The realistic answer requires either a Saturday departure with overnight camping or a less ambitious climb.

There's a deeper read across the three configurations on the back-calc that's worth naming. I'd given them two descent options with very different totals — downclimb at 2–3 hr, twelve rappels at 30 min each for ~6 hr — and asked for the leave time. That question is structurally under-specified: which descent matters because it shifts the answer by 3+ hours. The right behavior is to ask which one I'd take, or to give two leave times so I can pick. None of the three did. gpt-realtime-2 named both options out loud and then quoted a total that only fits the downclimb path. Gemini explicitly conflated them with an "or" that treats them as equivalent in time. This complicates the "S2S structurally caps reasoning" framing I've heard from voice builders. The shape was right on Gemini, the magnitudes were loose. ### Both models fabricated specifics, in different shapes

When I asked gpt-realtime-2 about the weather in Yosemite for the weekend, the model gave specific numbers: highs in the mid-80s, lows in the 50s, mostly sunny and dry. There's no live weather tool wired up to the session. The numbers are fabricated. The model didn't say "I can't check live forecasts, but typical conditions for that time of year are…" — it just answered with specifics a user might act on.

In the same session, a few turns later, I asked if it could do a web search and it correctly said it couldn't. So the model knows it doesn't have web search. It just doesn't always reach for that disclaimer when the question would benefit from one.

Gemini Live had a worse version of the same problem. When I asked it to check lodging, it said "Okay, looking into that now." When I asked about dispersed camping options, it said "Look into dispersed camping along Forest Service roads in the nearby Stanislaus or Inyo National Forests." Both phrasings imply a search is happening. No search was happening. I had to ask explicitly — "Are you looking?" — for the model to come back with "I'm providing information based on general knowledge."

The Yosemite weather case is the same underlying problem (no native tool composition for live data) producing a different surface failure (plausible specifics instead of obvious Python). The plausible failure is the more dangerous one because the user has no signal they should distrust it.

### Turn 5: the tool-use question, three different behaviors

The last turn of the script is deliberately designed to require a live lookup. Dark Star on Temple Crag is a real alpine route in the High Sierra; "ticks" on Mountain Project are climber logs that record recent conditions. Without a web search, the model can't answer correctly — what it does in that case is what I wanted to see.

> "I want to get on Dark Star on Temple Crag but I'm not sure what the weather is like in the High Sierras. Can you look into ticks on Mountain Project to see what the weather's like?"

The three configurations produced three distinct behaviors.

**gpt-realtime-2 didn't reach Turn 5 in this session.** After I pushed on "can you do a web search?" on a Turn 4 sub-question, the model went silent for three consecutive turns and the session derailed before I could ask the Dark Star question. On a different session three weeks earlier with the same shape of question — asking it to look up Mountain Project ticks — gpt-realtime-2 hallucinated a Python `requests.get()` call and spoke it aloud, then pretended to be processing while nothing was running. Two failure modes, same underlying problem: no native tool composition for live data, model improvises around the gap.

**Gemini Live refused cleanly with a redirect.** *"I cannot directly access specific 'ticks' or real-time conditions on Mountain Project. However, the High Sierra weather can be quite variable, so checking the general forecast for the elevation near Temple Crag is recommended."* Notable mostly because Gemini was *less* honest earlier in the same session — it had said "looking into that now" when it wasn't. The capability-disclosure behavior is inconsistent within a single session.

**Cascaded gave the best answer of the three.** *"I can't look up Mountain Project ticks directly, but for Temple Crag in summer you're generally looking at cool to mild days (often 50s–70s°F on route depending on wind/sun) and cold nights, with thunderstorms possible in the afternoon. I'd check the NOAA point forecast for Temple Crag / 3rd Lake plus recent MP comments for Dark Star to confirm snow, mosquitos, and storm patterns, and aim for a pre-dawn start to be off the upper face before any afternoon weather."*

It refuses the part it can't do, gives accurate seasonal knowledge a climber can actually use, names a specific NOAA point forecast and the right MP route page to check, and adds a tactical recommendation (pre-dawn start to beat afternoon weather). This is the response a climbing partner would give. It also names the limit explicitly up front, which Gemini did in this exchange and gpt-realtime-2 didn't on the 06-02 run.

The substance gap traces back to the model behind the LLM stage. GPT-5.1 over the Responses API is text-trained, has stronger calibration on what it doesn't know, and is willing to volunteer specific sources when it redirects. The S2S models default to either fabricating the action ("checking now…") or fabricating the data outright. That's a model behavior, not an API surface gap.

**The API surface gap is still real, though, and it's the cleanest fix.** The Realtime API's built-in tool surface is `function`, `mcp`, and built-in connectors (e.g. `connector_googlecalendar`, plus connectors for Drive, SharePoint, Dropbox, Box). The Responses API surface is much wider:

| Tool type | Responses API | Realtime API |
|---|---|---|
| `function` (custom logic) | ✓ | ✓ |
| `mcp` (incl. built-in connectors like `connector_googlecalendar`) | ✓ | ✓ |
| `web_search` | ✓ | ✗ |
| `file_search` | ✓ | ✗ |
| `code_interpreter` | ✓ | ✗ |
| `image_generation` | ✓ | ✗ |
| `computer_use` | ✓ | ✗ |

The function and MCP primitives are shared, so anything you can do as custom code or as an MCP server, you can do in both. What's missing on Realtime is the hosted-tool ecosystem that runs server-side in Responses: web search, file search, code interpreter, image generation. If you want any of those inside a voice agent today, you write a `function` tool that calls the Responses API for that capability and pipes the result back into the Realtime session. That works — community-documented — but the bridge adds latency, every developer writes it themselves, and the failure modes above (fabricated specifics, Python-as-voice, three silent turns) are all what happens when the bridge isn't there. Bringing the hosted tools in as native Realtime tool types is mostly plumbing, not new research.

### Transcript divergence from what the model heard

A smaller thing, more curious than broken. At one point I said "we'll rap down" — rappel — and the transcript layer rendered it as "wrap down." But the model's response a second later included "if you rappel 12 times at roughly 30 minutes each," so the model itself had heard "rap" correctly and understood the climbing context.

|---|---|

| **What I said (audio):** "rap down… 12 raps at 30 minutes per rap"<br/>**Transcript rendered:** "wrap down… 12 reps at 30 minutes per rep"<br/>**Model said:** "…if you rappel 12 times at roughly 30 minutes each, that's about 6 hours…" | **What I said (audio):** "rap down… 12 raps at 30 minutes per rap"<br/>**Transcript rendered:** "wrap down… 12 wraps at 30 minutes per wrap"<br/>**Model said:** "…2–3 hours for the downclimb or 12 rappels…" |

This is a property of S2S models that doesn't exist in cascaded pipelines: the transcript and the speech are generated as parallel channels off the same audio embedding. They can disagree, because the transcript is its own decoding head, not the input to the LLM. In cascaded, the transcript IS the input — if it mishears "rap" as "wrap," the model gets "wrap" and answers about packaging. In S2S, the audio carries through, and the displayed transcript is closer to a debug view than ground truth.

Later in the Gemini session, the same shape: I asked about "the weather in the high series" — transcript rendered exactly that — and Gemini responded about "High Sierra weather." Cross-model finding: this is an S2S-class property, not a quirk of one vendor's pipeline.

I also saw the same shape earlier in a different session with gpt-realtime-2: I said "I'm going to Bishop" (the Eastern Sierra town) and the transcript rendered "worship," but the model correctly responded about Bishop, California. The displayed transcript was wrong; the answer was right.

This is the one S2S advantage that survives every comparison in my testing. It's also currently invisible to the developer — there's no API field for "what the model actually understood from the audio," only the transcript that may or may not match.

## Closing

I'm going to keep using S2S over cascaded for casual conversational use the way I'd previously used ChatGPT voice mode. The latency floor and barge-in are real wins, and the audio-preservation property catches transcript mistakes that would otherwise derail the answer.

Where I wouldn't put either S2S model in front of a customer yet: anything that has to be reliable. Both Realtime and Gemini Live dropped a date constraint inside the same six-turn conversation, both fabricated specifics on questions that needed a live lookup (gpt-realtime-2 silently, Gemini Live by claiming to be searching when it wasn't), both went silent for several consecutive turns in this session under different triggers, and both silently resolved an under-specified constraint in the back-calculation rather than asking. These are the same shapes of failure across two different vendors' speech-to-speech stacks, which makes them feel less like vendor quirks and more like where the modality is right now.

The fixes look like they live in two different places. The web search gap on Realtime is the cleanest API-surface fix — bringing the Responses hosted-tool ecosystem in as native Realtime tool types is plumbing, not new research, and it would cut off most of the failure modes above at the root. The other gaps — context retention across turns, calibration on when to volunteer a "I can't actually check this" disclaimer, asking instead of silently picking when a constraint is under-specified — are model-training gaps that the cascaded GPT-5.1 baseline doesn't share. Both companies will close them. Worth checking back in a few months.
